# Ivory Nikah — Reference Analysis

## 1. Overall Design Identity

The Ivory Nikah reference presents a pair of ornate Mughal palace double doors carved from ivory/cream marble, framed by a multi-lobed mehrab arch. The design language is one of restrained opulence — every surface carries intricate bas-relief jasmine vine carvings and gold inlay trim, yet the monochromatic ivory-and-gold palette keeps the composition serene rather than overwhelming. The effect is of walking through the entrance of a Mughal royal pavilion: architecturally grand, botanically rich, and suffused with warm natural daylight that softens the carved stone into something almost alive. This is a Nikah invitation that says "sacred threshold" — the couple invites guests to step through these palace doors into a celebration of faith and union.

---

## 2. Composition & Layout

### Structure
- **Central axis symmetry:** Two identical door panels meet at a vertical center line, forming a bilateral composition.
- **Framing hierarchy:** Outer rectangular pilaster columns > scalloped mehrab arch > upper floral tympanum > door panels > lower arabesque panels.
- **Vertical thirds:** The composition divides roughly into upper arch zone (~25%), main door panels with knocker (~50%), and lower dense-carving panels (~25%).

### Proportions
- The overall aspect ratio is approximately **3:5 (portrait)**, well suited to a mobile-first scrolling invitation.
- The arch opening occupies about 65% of the total width, with pillars flanking at ~17.5% each side.
- Door panels are vertically bisected by the gold knocker ring, which sits at roughly the golden ratio point (~0.618 from the top of the door panel).

### Hierarchy of attention
1. Gold lotus door knocker rings (focal point, highest contrast)
2. Scalloped mehrab arch silhouette (distinctive shape, establishes cultural identity immediately)
3. Jasmine vine carvings (fills the eye after the focal point, draws attention upward and downward)
4. Jali lattice pillars (peripheral framing, noticed last)

### Implications for template
- Opening animation should reveal content through the arch opening, with doors as the cinematic gate.
- Content sections should sit within the arch silhouette conceptually, with floral borders framing each section.
- The vertical scroll maps naturally to "descending through the palace" — arch at top, details below.

## 3. Architecture & Decorative Elements

### Mughal Mehrab Arch
- **Type:** Multi-lobed / scalloped cusped arch (7 lobes visible), a signature of Mughal palatial architecture (Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Agra Fort).
- **Construction:** Three concentric arch bands — outer carved floral band, middle gold-trimmed band, inner smooth ivory band. This layered depth creates a sense of receding space.
- **Cultural context:** The mehrab form originates from mosque prayer niches (mihrab) indicating the direction of Mecca, but evolved in Mughal secular architecture as a framing motif for doorways, windows, and jharokhas. Its use here references sacred geometry while remaining appropriate for a wedding context.

### Door Panels
- **Upper panel:** Jasmine vine arabesque in bas-relief, with a central pointed-arch cartouche (a smaller mehrab echo within the door).
- **Middle feature:** Gold lotus-bud door knocker with ring, set within a pointed trefoil frame. The lotus is a Mughal symbol of purity and divine beauty.
- **Lower panel:** Denser, more tightly wound floral vine arabesque — creates visual weight at the base, grounding the composition.
- **Edge trim:** Continuous gold bead-and-reel molding runs vertically along the door meeting edge, with small gold rosette studs at regular intervals.

### Side Pillars (Pilasters)
- **Jali lattice panels:** Geometric interlocking star-and-cross pattern typical of Mughal jali screens (Fatehpur Sikri, Humayun's Tomb). These are carved stone screens that historically allowed light and air to pass through while maintaining privacy.
- **Column details:** Simple carved acanthus-leaf capitals and plain square bases. The columns are engaged (flat-backed against the wall), not freestanding.
- **Proportions:** Slender compared to the doors, roughly 1:6 width-to-height ratio.

### Floral Tympanum (above arch)
- Jasmine sprays fan outward from the arch crown, filling the rectangular spandrel area above the scalloped arch. This is a typical Mughal compositional device — nature contained within geometry.

### Color relationships
- The palette is **monochromatic warm** with two accent channels: gold (metallic warmth) and sage (natural coolness).
- Gold appears at every scale — from the massive knocker to the finest trim lines — creating visual continuity.
- Sage green is subordinate — it exists only in the botanical carvings and never competes with the gold.
- The overall temperature is **warm-neutral**, approximately 3500K color temperature equivalent.
- Shadow play adds the only true darks; without it, the palette would be almost entirely above 70% lightness.

## 5. Typography Direction

The reference contains no text, so typography must be inferred from the design's period, formality, and cultural context.

### Recommended Google Fonts

| Role | Font | Weight | Rationale |
|------|------|--------|-----------|
| Couple names (display) | **Playfair Display** | 700 | High-contrast didone serifs echo the formal engraving quality of Mughal stone inscriptions. The vertical stress complements the arch's vertical axis. |
| Section headings | **Cormorant Garamond** | 600 SemiBold | Elegant old-style serif with calligraphic quality. More refined than Playfair at mid-sizes. |
| Body text | **Lato** | 400 Regular, 300 Light | Clean humanist sans-serif for readability at small sizes. Neutral enough not to compete with decorative elements. |
| Arabic / Urdu script | **Amiri** | 400 Regular | Naskh-style Arabic typeface designed for readability. Appropriate for Bismillah and Quranic verses. Harmonizes with the Mughal aesthetic. |

### Typography styling cues
- **Letter-spacing:** Generous tracking on display text (0.05–0.1em) to match the spaciousness of the carved surfaces.
- **Text color:** Deep brown `#3D2B1F` for primary text; warm brown `#6B5B4E` for secondary/body. Gold `#C9A84C` reserved for decorative lines and small accents only (not body text — insufficient contrast).
- **Alignment:** Centered, following the bilateral symmetry of the reference.
- **Case:** Display titles in title case; avoid all-caps except for very short labels (2-3 words maximum).

## 6. Floral & Botanical Elements

### Jasmine (Mogra / Arabian Jasmine)
- **Appearance:** 5-petal star-shaped blooms, approximately 2-3cm in the carving scale. Pure white petals with a slightly warm tint from the marble medium. Tiny clustered stamens at center, rendered as small gold dots in the relief.
- **Cultural significance:** Jasmine (mogra) is the quintessential South Asian wedding flower. In Muslim wedding traditions, jasmine garlands are exchanged, and the flower symbolizes purity, love, and grace. Its fragrance is associated with paradise in Islamic tradition.
- **Rendering style:** The carvings show jasmine in a **Mughal pietra dura / bas-relief** style — naturalistic but idealized. Flowers face forward (heraldic presentation), stems curve organically.

### Vine & Leaf System
- **Vine:** Continuous sinuous S-curve stem, typical of the "arabesque" — an infinitely repeating vine that symbolizes eternal life in Islamic art.
- **Leaves:** Elongated pointed oval, arranged in alternating pairs along the vine. Rendered in sage green (`#8A9A7B`), with visible veining suggesting naturalism.
- **Buds:** Smaller unopened jasmine buds punctuate the vine between full blooms, adding visual rhythm and suggesting growth/anticipation.

### Compositional patterns
- **Door panels:** Ascending vine from lower-left to upper-right (reversed on opposite door), creating a V-shaped upward movement when both doors are viewed together.
- **Lower panels:** Tighter, denser vine pattern with more leaves and fewer open blooms — a "thicket" effect that grounds the composition.
- **Tympanum:** Outward-fanning sprays, creating a crown/halo effect above the arch.

### Template implications
- Floral vine borders should use the same continuous-arabesque pattern for section dividers.
- Corner ornaments should echo the carved relief style — not flat illustration.
- Floating petal animations should use the warm-white jasmine petal tone, not pure white.

## 7. Cultural Markers & Symbolism

### Islamic / Mughal Elements
| Element | Significance |
|---------|-------------|
| Mehrab arch | Sacred threshold; echoes mosque mihrab; frames the invitation as a spiritual doorway |
| Jali lattice | Islamic geometric perfection; unity of mathematical order and beauty; historically marks private/sacred spaces |
| Arabesque vines | Infinite pattern symbolizing eternal life; Islamic prohibition on figurative art led to the flowering of botanical and geometric ornament |
| Lotus door knocker | Purity and divine beauty; the lotus is shared symbolism between Hindu and Islamic Mughal traditions, representing the union of cultures |
| Gold inlay | Royal patronage; Mughal emperors were renowned for gold-inlaid marble (pietra dura) at the Taj Mahal, Itmad-ud-Daulah |
| Bilateral symmetry | Order, balance, divine perfection — a fundamental principle of Islamic art and architecture |

### Wedding-Specific Symbolism
- **Double doors:** The threshold metaphor — two lives joining as one, the couple entering a new chapter together.
- **The open arch:** Invitation, welcome, hospitality — central to Nikah celebrations where the community witnesses the union.
- **Ascending vines:** Growth, flourishing of the marriage, the building of a family.
- **Warm daylight:** Blessing, divine favor (noor/light is a central concept in Islamic theology).

### Template implementation
- Include Bismillah (In the name of Allah) as the opening invocation.
- Support Quranic dua (supplication) text, likely the marriage dua from Surah Al-Furqan 25:74.
- Ceremony names: Mehndi, Nikah, Walima (not generic "ceremony/reception").
- The door-opening animation is not just decorative — it enacts the threshold metaphor.

## 8. Lighting & Atmosphere

### Light source
- **Direction:** Upper-left at approximately 30-40 degrees from vertical, consistent with natural daylight through an opening or window.
- **Quality:** Soft and diffused — no hard shadows. This suggests indirect sunlight, as would filter into a covered palace courtyard or pavilion.
- **Cast shadows:** Visible on the left-hand door's upper area (palm/foliage shadow patterns from outside), adding organic texture to the carved surface.

### Shadow behavior
- **Relief shadows:** Subtle, shallow shadows beneath each carved petal and leaf, giving the flat image its three-dimensional quality. The shadow direction is consistent (upper-left light).
- **Recess shadows:** Darker tones in the arch opening, behind the jali lattice, and in the deeper carved channels — these create depth layers.
- **Ground shadow:** Not visible (doors appear to be photographed straight-on).

### Atmospheric effects
- **Warm color cast:** The entire scene has a warm golden-hour tone, approximately 3200-3500K. This is not neutral daylight but "palace light" — sunlight filtered through warm stone.
- **Subtle glow:** The carved white marble surfaces have a slight luminous quality, as if the stone absorbs and re-emits light. This should translate to CSS as subtle inner-glow effects on decorative elements.
- **Dust motes / atmosphere:** Not visible in the reference, but the warmth suggests the possibility of subtle animated particle effects (floating jasmine petals, warm light rays).

### Template animation cues
- Warm light gradient shifting subtly over time (simulating passage of sunlight).
- Parallax shadow layers on decorative elements to maintain 3D carved feel during scroll.
- Floating jasmine petal particles in the warm-white to cream color range.
- Avoid cool-toned effects — everything should remain in the warm spectrum.

## 9. Materials & Textures

### Primary material: Carved marble
- **Surface:** Smooth, slightly polished but not mirror-reflective. The surface has a satin/matte quality with very subtle grain.
- **Color variation:** Not uniform white — the marble shows gentle warm veining and slight color shifts, giving it organic character.
- **Carving depth:** Two levels of relief visible — shallow (leaf veining, fine vine tendrils) and medium (full flower forms, arch moldings). No deep undercut carving.
- **Template translation:** The marble-texture.jpg background asset should be used at reduced opacity (15-25%) beneath content areas to give surfaces material presence without competing with text.

### Secondary material: Gold metalwork
- **Finish:** Burnished/satin gold — not mirror-bright, not matte. Has warmth and glow without being flashy.
- **Application:** Appears as inlay within carved channels (trim lines), cast forms (knocker, rosettes), and edge banding (door frame molding).
- **Template translation:** Gold elements should use CSS gradients or subtle texture to avoid looking flat. A linear-gradient from `#C9A84C` to `#D5BD91` simulates the burnished quality.

### Tertiary: Stone (jali lattice)
- **Material:** Same marble as the doors but with deeper carving creating a see-through screen effect.
- **Transparency:** The jali has true voids (black/transparent areas) where the pattern is cut through. This transparency is important for overlaying the lattice on content.
- **Template translation:** The jali-lattice.png asset preserves this transparency on its alpha channel and can be used as overlay masks or decorative borders at reduced opacity.

### Surface aging / patina
- The reference shows pristine condition — no visible wear, staining, or aging. This suggests the template should aim for a "new palace" feel: clean, luminous, and pristine rather than weathered or vintage.
