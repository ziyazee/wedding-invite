# Muslim Wedding: Cultural Direction (Hyderabadi / Deccani, Sunni)

Classification tags: **[H]** historically grounded · **[M]** modern interpretation, widely accepted · **[D]** decorative choice · **[?]** uncertain or varies by family, so confirm with the client.
Sample: Zayd Ahmed Khan & Amira Fatima Siddiqui. Events: Mehendi & Dholak Soirée (Haveli Gulzar), Nikah (Chowmahalla Palace), Walima (Falaknuma Palace).

## 1. Ceremonies and sequence
In a Sunni (mostly Hanafi in the Deccan) wedding, the nikah is a legal contract made before witnesses and blessed with prayer. It is not a sacrament performed around fire or idols. Families treat it as a joyful sunnah ("half of the deen").
- **Sanchaq [H/?]:** the groom's family carries trays (thaal) of the bride's wedding outfit, jewellery, mehendi, dry fruit and sweets to her home. Some families merge it into the mehendi.
- **Manjha [H]:** a Hyderabadi pre-wedding ritual. The bride wears yellow and turmeric paste (ubtan) is applied. She stays at home in quiet seclusion before the wedding, and women sing around her. In the Hyderabadi context, call it *manjha*, not "haldi".
- **Mehendi & dholak [H]:** women of both families gather. Henna goes on the bride's hands and feet, and the women sing *dholak ke geet* (teasing folk songs) to the dholak drum. The mood is intimate, green-and-gold, floral and playful. Some families hide the groom's initials in the bride's henna [M].
- **Nikah [H]:** usually led by a qazi, sometimes in a mosque or with men and women seated separately. It has five parts:
  1. **Khutbat-un-nikah:** a short sermon reciting Qur'an 4:1, 3:102 and 33:70–71, which counsel piety and kindness between spouses.
  2. **Ijab-o-qubool:** the offer and acceptance, said three times ("qubool hai"). The bride consents, often through a wakil (representative). Two adult witnesses are required.
  3. **Mehr:** an obligatory gift from groom to bride. It is her right and her property, recorded in the **nikahnama** (marriage contract), which is then signed.
  4. **Dua:** the families pray for the couple. A common wedding blessing is *Barakallahu laka wa baraka 'alaika wa jama'a bainakuma fi khair*.
  5. **Celebration:** chhuhare (dried dates) and mishri (rock sugar) are distributed, then guests embrace and congratulate the families (mubarakbad).
- **Jalwa / Arsi Mushaf [H/?]:** right after the nikah the couple sit together under one dupatta. They see each other through a mirror (arsi), with a Qur'an (mushaf) placed nearby. This custom is shared across Deccani and North-Indian Muslim families, but not every family observes it.
- **Rukhsati [H]:** the bride's farewell from her parents' home. It is emotional, and the bride's family may hold a Qur'an over her head as she leaves.
- **Walima [H]:** the groom's feast, a sunnah (Prophetic practice) that publicly announces the marriage. In Hyderabad it is the grand palace evening: biryani, *qubani ka meetha* (apricot dessert), *double ka meetha* (bread pudding) and paan. Its meaning is gratitude and hospitality.
- **Chauthi [H/?]:** a post-wedding visit to the bride's home. It is optional, so leave it out of the template.

## 2. Visual vocabulary
**Geometry and architecture**
- **Khatam (8-pointed star) [H]:** two squares overlapping at 45°. It is the base unit of girih and tile patterns. Never use a 6-pointed star (see section 3).
- **Girih strapwork [H]:** interlaced polygonal bands. Use it for dividers, frames and lattice fills at low opacity.
- **Islimi (arabesque) [H]:** endless split-leaf scrolls. Use it on card borders and vines, and keep it abstract and vegetal.
- **Muqarnas [H]:** stalactite vaulting found in Qutb Shahi tombs and gateways. Use it as stepped top edges for headers.
- **Deccani arches [H]:** pointed four-centred arches with cusped (multifoil) edges and stucco medallions. The Charminar (1591) and Mecca Masjid follow this form, and the Chowmahalla courtyards use scalloped versions. Ogee arches are acceptable [D].
- **Jali [H]:** pierced stone screens. Use them as light-through-lattice overlays, a metaphor for gentle unveiling.
- **Mihrab-like niche [H/D]:** use it only as an architectural frame shape. Never make it read as an actual prayer niche, so put no qibla cues and no lamp-in-niche Quranic iconography inside it.
- **Hyderabadi landmarks:**
  - **Charminar silhouette [H]:** one restrained landmark mark, not wallpaper.
  - **Chowmahalla [H]:** Asaf Jahi palace with arcaded white façades, chandeliers and fountain courts.
  - **Falaknuma [H]:** built by Nawab Viqar-ul-Umra in a European neoclassical style. Show it with chandeliers, a grand staircase and a Venetian-glass mood, not with Islamic arches.

**Crafts and objects**
- **Bidri ware [H]:** from Bidar in the Deccan. Blackened zinc alloy inlaid with silver in poppy and vine motifs. Good for dark accent cards.
- **Nizami zardozi [H]:** gold metal-thread embroidery that suits the brass linework. The ambient "zari fleck" sprite comes from this.
- **Hyderabadi pearls [H]:** the Nizam pearl tradition. Draw on the *satlada* (seven-strand pearl necklace) and pearl tassels.
- **Kalamkari [?]:** choose with care. The Srikalahasti style is figurative Hindu mythology, so avoid it. The Machilipatnam block-print style has Persianate florals; use it for florals only.
- **Brass and silver objects [H]:**
  - itardan (perfume holder), paandan (betel box) and gulabpash (rose-water sprinkler);
  - aftaba-chilamchi (ewer and basin for hand-washing before the feast);
  - fanoos (lantern) and chandelier light.
  - Use lanterns and electric glow only. No oil diyas.

**Flora [H]**
- Gulab (rose) and its petals, also used as a rose-petal chaadar (floral sheet).
- Mogra, chameli (jasmine) and rajnigandha (tuberose), used in gajra (wrist and hair garlands) and the sehra (groom's floral veil).
- Mehndi leaf (henna) as a sprig motif.
- Hyderabadi bridal flowers are white and red, with heavy mogra.

**Textiles [H]**
- **Bride:** khada dupatta (a 6-yard dupatta draped over kurta and churidar), the Hyderabadi bridal signature. A sharara or gharara is also common. Jewellery includes satlada, jugni, chandbali, tika/jhoomar and nath.
- **Groom:** sherwani, dastar/safa (turban) and a mogra sehra.

**Food and ritual objects [H]**
- Dates and dry fruit on a thaal (tray), chhuhare, mishri
- Attar bottles and gulabpash
- Mehndi cone, gajra
- Dholak drum
- Mirror (arsi) and nikahnama scroll

## 3. Calligraphy and religious-symbol guardrails
**Sacred text**
- Never place Allah, Muhammad (ﷺ) or any Qur'anic verse on anything stepped on, cut, cropped by a mask, tiled as a pattern, scattered as particles, animated letter by letter, used as a background texture or placed behind other content.
- An optional *Bismillah* line may go once, at the top of the Opening or Couple section, as plain respectful type. It is common on Muslim invites. Use a toggle and default it on only if the client wants it [M].
- Render the dua only as plain static type, with an English translation and a review note. Native-speaker or scholar review is required before shipping any Arabic or Urdu text.

**Imagery**
- Decorative assets must contain no human or animal figures (aniconic). That rules out birds, peacocks, elephants, horses, couple silhouettes and the doli carried by people. Client photos in media slots are the client's choice.

**Symbols**
- **Khatam:** draw it as two rotated squares. The *rub el hizb* (khatam with a circle) marks sections of the Qur'an, so use it sparingly and never as confetti.
- **Crescent:** use it at most once (for example the Closing moon). No crescent-and-star spam, no flag-like crescents, and no Ramadan clichés (crescent, lantern and dates together read as "Eid sale").
- **Lanterns:** read them as Hyderabadi wedding light, not Ramadan.

**Avoid**
- **North-Indian Hindu tropes:** kalash, diya or aarti, Om, swastika, ganesha, pheras or the agni mandap fire, sindoor, mangalsutra, toran with mango leaves, haldi-as-Hindu-rite, varmala framing, coconut, shehnai-at-temple.
- **Shia-specific emblems** (this is a Sunni family): alam, panja (hand), and imam zamin, which is common in the Deccan but often Shia-coded [?].
- **Other cues:** generic Arab or Gulf imagery (camels, dunes, onion-dome mosques with minarets, the Kaaba), alcohol or champagne glasses, and dancing-couple imagery. Treat music as opt-in (the existing MusicButton is fine).

## 4. Section-by-section asset vocabulary
**Couple / Families**
- *Cusped Deccani arch* as the portrait frame: the threshold of a new home.
- *Jali screen* fading open: modesty and gentle unveiling.
- *Arsi mirror* (round brass mirror): the jalwa moment of first seeing each other.
- *Mogra sehra strands* hanging from the arch: the groom's honour.
- *Satlada pearl strand* linking the two family names: lineage and blessing.

**Countdown**
- *Brass fanoos* whose glow brightens as the day nears: anticipation and a lit home.
- *Muqarnas* digit frames: patience built tier by tier.
- *Khatam* rosette as the clock face, with eight points standing for completeness.
- *Itardan* with drops: the fragrance of the coming day.

**Celebrations: Mehendi**
- *Mehndi cone* and *mehndi-leaf sprig*: adornment and joy.
- *Dholak*: women's songs.
- *Gajra*: bridal adornment.
- *Turmeric/yellow manjha bowl* (brass): purification.
- *Thaal of sanchaq trays*: gifts between families.

**Celebrations: Nikah**
- *Nikahnama scroll and qalam (pen)*: the sacred contract.
- *Thaal of chhuhare and mishri*: sweetness shared.
- *White-rose stage arch*: purity, matching the Opening film.
- *Sealed mehr pouch or box*: the bride's right.
- Use a *Chowmahalla arcade* outline only.

**Celebrations: Walima**
- *Falaknuma chandelier*: grand hospitality.
- *Aftaba-chilamchi*: welcoming guests to the meal.
- *Silver or bidri paandan*: the Hyderabadi ending to a feast.
- *Biryani deg (pot) outline*.
- *Gulabpash*: rose water sprinkled on arriving guests.

**Celebrations: generic fallback**
- Rose, lantern, attar bottle, khatam medallion, and a cusped-arch card.

**RSVP**
- *Gulabpash*: a welcoming gesture.
- *Paandan*: hospitality.
- *Brass ewer*: readiness to receive.
- *Sealed envelope with khatam wax seal*.
- *Open jali gate*: "you are welcome inside".

**Closing**
- *Single crescent over the Charminar silhouette*: Hyderabad at night, home.
- *Rose-petal chaadar*: blessings showered.
- *Lantern dimming to stars*.
- *Dua in plain type*.
- *Mogra garland* closing the frame.

## 6. Palette and typography
**Palette.** Keep the existing tokens (alabaster `bg #F6F0E6`, brass `gold #A87C2D`, walnut ink, sage `teal #586B53`). Add three accent tokens:
- `rose: #8E3B4A` (deep gulab): mehendi accents and RSVP highlights. Prefer it over vermilion, which reads as sindoor.
- `jade: #1F4B47` (midnight teal-jade): bidri-like night panels in Closing and Walima. Avoid flag green (#00843D-ish).
- `pearl: #EFE9DE` with a `silver: #A9A9A4` stroke: satlada, bidri inlay and Nikah whites.

Use manjha yellow (`#D9B44A`) only inside the Mehendi card. Keep red-and-gold saturation low so the template does not read as a Hindu bridal invite.

**Typography.** Cormorant Garamond with Plus Jakarta Sans works well. For Arabic or Urdu lines:
- *Urdu* needs **Nastaliq**: Gulzar or Noto Nastaliq Urdu (Google Fonts). Do not set Urdu in a Naskh font.
- *Arabic* (Bismillah, dua): Amiri or Scheherazade New for Naskh. Reem Kufi for a geometric Kufic accent, but only on non-sacred words.

**Arabic-script cautions**
- Set `dir="rtl"` and `lang="ar"` or `lang="ur"`.
- Never use letter-spacing, faux italic or bold, or uppercase transforms.
- Never split by character or word for animation (the template's `Words.tsx` splitting must not touch Arabic or Urdu). Fade the whole line.
- Give Nastaliq a generous line-height (≈2.0–2.2) and do not crop descenders or ascenders.
- Keep diacritics (harakat) intact. Use the correct code points (Urdu ی/ک/ہ versus Arabic ي/ك/ه), and do not hand-type ﷺ ligatures unless the font supports them.
- Avoid machine translation; every Arabic or Urdu string needs a native-speaker review.

## 7. Sources and confidence
- **Known Islamic practice:** nikah elements, khutbah verses, mehr, witnesses, walima sunnah, and the Hanafi norms most common in the Deccan.
- **Documented Hyderabadi custom:** manjha, sanchaq, khada dupatta, satlada, jalwa/arsi mushaf, qubani ka meetha, paan, bidri (Bidar), Qutb Shahi and Asaf Jahi architecture (Charminar, Mecca Masjid, Chowmahalla), and Falaknuma's European style.
- **Uncertain or varies by family [?]:** ritual names and order, chauthi, hidden initials in henna, Kalamkari suitability, imam zamin. Confirm with the client before using copy that depends on them.
