# Punjabi Journey: Style DNA (image-skill method)

Why: the first test scene read as a smooth AI render (glossy, photoreal volume, one blended image). A 6-scene journey needs one hand-made, specific look that also suits parallax.

1. Core look: a Kangra/Punjab-school miniature painting rebuilt as a paper theatre. Each plane is a separate hand-painted cut-out sheet.
2. Material language: opaque gouache on handmade cotton paper, visible paper tooth and fibre, fine sable-brush ink outlines (siyahi qalam), flat colour fields with dry-brush tonal shifts, gold-leaf highlights on finials and borders, Amritsar-style fresco floral panels, phulkari stitch geometry in textiles.
3. Structural rule: planes stay flat and graphic. Depth comes from overlap and scale between sheets, never from glossy 3D lighting, bloom, or photoreal blur.
4. Palette: mustard #E3A82B, vermilion #C23B22, phulkari magenta #B0225B, parrot green #3E7C3A, cream paper #F4E6C6, kiln terracotta #B5573A, gold leaf #C9972B, night indigo #1E2A5A (indigo only on planes keyed on green).

Reject checklist (re-prompt naming the failure, do not patch):
- smooth gradient render, plastic sheen, HDR bloom, photographic depth of field
- uniform digital noise instead of paper tooth
- outlines missing (looks like vector clip art) or heavy black cartoon outlines
- generic Taj/Rajasthan palace instead of Punjabi haveli (flat roofs with chhatris, brick+fresco, phulkari)
- sacred imagery, figures, text, logos, watermark
- key-colour spill or key colour inside the subject
- planes that disagree on light (sun is always low, from the right, warm)

Key colours: warm planes on #0057FF, indigo-heavy planes on #00D26A.
