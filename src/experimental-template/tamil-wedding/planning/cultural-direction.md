# Tamil Wedding (Thirumanam / Kalyanam) — Cultural Direction

Template: `tamil-wedding`
Scope: Tamil Nadu Hindu wedding, broadly pan-community, with Chettinad architecture as the visual setting.

**Classification key:**
- **[H]** Historically grounded: a documented traditional element with cultural lineage.
- **[M]** Modern interpretation: a contemporary practice that is widely accepted.
- **[D]** Decorative choice: fits the look but is not specific to this tradition.
- **⚠** Uncertain, or varies by community. Confirm with the couple before using.

---

## 1. Cultural overview

- A Tamil Hindu wedding is called *thirumanam* (திருமணம்) or *kalyanam* (கல்யாணம்). The central rite is *mangalya dharanam*: the groom ties the *thaali* at the auspicious hour (*muhurtham*) while the musicians play *getti melam*.
- Customs differ a lot between communities. Brahmin weddings (Iyer and Iyengar) include *Vratham*, *Kasi Yathrai*, *Oonjal* and *Saptapadi*. Non-Brahmin communities (for example Chettiar, Mudaliar, Pillai, Gounder, Thevar and Nadar) have their own *seer* (gift exchange) customs, and their thaali designs differ.
- **Keep generic copy community-neutral.** Brahmin-only rites should be optional text that the couple can switch on.
- *Suyamariyathai* (Self-Respect) marriages are legally recognised in Tamil Nadu and do not involve a priest. A secular wording variant should remain possible. [H]
- Setting choice: the Chettinad mansion (Nattukottai Chettiar heritage, Karaikudi region) and the *agraharam* (a Brahmin row-house street) are **different worlds**. Use one. Do not blend them into a single "Tamil house".

## 2. Sacred invocations and auspicious marks

| Element | Use | Class |
|---|---|---|
| **உ** (*Pillaiyar Suzhi*) | A Ganesha invocation written at the top of any auspicious document. Place it once, top-centre, above everything else. | [H] |
| **சிவமயம்** (*Sivamayam*) | A Shaiva line placed under **உ** | [H] ⚠ depends on sect |
| **ஸ்ரீ** / **ஸ்ரீ ராமஜெயம்** / **ஸ்ரீ வேங்கடேச துணை** | Vaishnava or family-deity alternatives to *Sivamayam* | [H] ⚠ |
| **[family deity] துணை** | "With the grace of [the family deity]" | [H] — make it an editable field |
| **திருமண அழைப்பிதழ்** | "Wedding invitation", used as a heading | [H] |
| **சுப முகூர்த்தம்** | "Auspicious hour", used as the label for the muhurtham time | [H] |
| **கல்யாண பத்திரிகை** | The traditional name for the invitation letter | [H] |

**Placement rules**
- **உ** and deity names appear **once**, top-centre, above the names. Never in the footer, never at the bottom of a scroll, and never under the couple's names.
- Never use them as particles, repeating patterns, loading spinners, bullet points or watermarks.
- Never crop, mask, rotate, animate them away, or let other elements overlap them. A gentle fade-in or a gold-leaf shimmer is acceptable.
- Never place them on a floor plane, a doormat, a threshold kolam or any surface people walk on.
- Leave room in the layout for a single yellow-turmeric and red-kumkum dot at the invitation's corner or crest. Traditionally the printed card itself is marked this way. [H]

## 3. Sacred architecture and household heritage

**The Chettinad mansion** [H]
- *Thinnai* (திண்ணை): a raised platform along the front verandah where guests are received. A good place to stage the entry or opening scene.
- *Valavu*: the central open-sky courtyard of a Chettinad house. *Mutram* (முற்றம்) is the general Tamil word for courtyard, and an agraharam house has a smaller *mutram*. Light and rain fall into the centre.
- **Athangudi tiles** are handmade cement tiles from Athangudi village. They come in deep ochre, oxide red, peacock teal, mustard and black, with geometric and floral patterns. They make a strong floor or background pattern. [H]
- **Burma-teak pillars**: dark, polished and carved. Granite column bases are also common.
- Other details: wooden doors with brass studs, Belgian mirrors, Italian marble, egg-plaster (*muttai chunnambu*) walls, coloured glass, and a colonnaded courtyard. [H]

**The Kalyana Mandapam and pandal**
- **Fruiting plantain trees** (*vazhai maram*, வாழை மரம்), with fruit bunches and the hanging flower (*vazhaipoo*), are tied to the entrance pillars. They signify fertility and an unbroken family line. [H]
- *Manavarai*: the decorated wedding platform where the rites take place. [H]
- *Pandhal* (பந்தல்): the canopy. *Pandhakaal* is its first post, decorated with mango leaves and turmeric. [H]
- The *Oonjal* (a swing hung on chains) is used in the Oonjal rite. [H, mainly Brahmin]

**Temple forms**
- The *gopuram* is the tall **gateway tower**: a trapezoid of stepped tiers crowded with sculptures, capped by a barrel-vault roof with a row of *kalasam* finials.
- The *vimanam* is the tower over the **inner sanctum**: shorter and more pyramidal, topped by a single dome-like *shikharam* and one *kalasam*.
- **Do not mix the two up.** Use gopuram silhouettes for borders and gateway frames, not for the sanctum. [H]
- A frame shaped like a *thiruvachi* (the arch behind a deity, with *yali* and *makara* heads) suits portraits. [H; using it for the couple's portrait is a modern-decorative choice]

## 4. Ritual objects, lamps and vessels

| Object | Visual notes | Class |
|---|---|---|
| **Kuthuvilakku** (குத்துவிளக்கு) | A tall brass standing lamp: round base, slender shaft, oil bowl with **five wicks** (*panchamukha*), and an *annapakshi* (swan) finial. A peacock finial is a valid alternative. Light it in pairs on either side of the manavarai. Flames warm and still. | [H] |
| **Purna Kumbham / Kalasam** | A brass or silver pot holding water, ringed with five or seven mango leaves, topped by a turmeric-smeared coconut, often standing on a heap of paddy or rice. Turmeric and kumkum dots on the pot. | [H] |
| **Thaali / Thirumangalyam** | A pendant on *manjal kayiru* (மஞ்சள் கயிறு), a turmeric-dyed thread. **The pendant's design depends on community** (Iyer, Iyengar, Chettiar *kazhuthiru*, and others). Draw it abstractly or as an editable variant, never as a single "Tamil thaali". | [H] ⚠ |
| **Kungumam & Manjal** | Vermilion and turmeric, served in a silver *kungumam chimizh* (சிமிழ், a small lidded box) or on a tray. Offered to married women guests. | [H] |
| **Panneer Sombu** (பன்னீர் சொம்பு) | A silver rosewater sprinkler: a long narrow neck with a perforated tip. Often paired with a *sandhanam kinnam* (sandalwood-paste bowl). Used to welcome guests at the entrance. | [H] |
| **Thamboolam** | A tray of betel leaves, areca nut, banana, coconut and a turmeric root. Return gifts to guests. | [H] |
| **Metti** | Silver toe rings put on the bride during *Ammi Midhithal* (stepping on the grinding stone). | [H] |
| **Pachai pidi** | Rice balls coloured red and yellow, circled around the couple during the Oonjal rite to ward off the evil eye. | [H, Brahmin] |

## 5. Textiles and craft

**Kanchipuram silk** (*Kanchi pattu*), a GI-tagged craft from Kanchipuram [H]
- Pure mulberry silk woven with real zari (silver thread gilded with gold). It is heavy and has a sheen that changes colour with the light.
- **Korvai**: a technique that interlocks a border of a contrasting colour with the body of the sari. The borders are woven separately and joined. Classic contrasts: arakku red with mayil green, mustard with maroon, rani pink with gold.
- Motifs:
  - *mayil* (peacock)
  - *annam / annapakshi* (hamsa swan)
  - *yali* (mythical lion)
  - *rudraksham* (rows of beads)
  - *mallimoggu* (jasmine buds)
  - *thazhampoo* (screw-pine)
  - *kamalam* (lotus)
  - *kattam* (checks)
  - a **temple border** of stepped triangles in the gopuram form, often called "temple" or "gopuram" border
- **Koorai pudavai**: the bridal sari gifted by the groom's family and worn for the thaali tying. In Brahmin weddings it is the nine-yard *madisar*, often arakku red or checked. [H]
- **Groom**: a cream *pattu veshti* with a zari border, plus an *angavastram* (shoulder cloth). Brahmin grooms wear the *panchakacham* drape. [H]

**Kolam** (கோலம்) [H]
- Rice-flour designs drawn by hand on the threshold at dawn. They invite prosperity and once fed ants and birds.
- Main forms:
  - *Pulli kolam*: lines drawn around a grid of dots.
  - *Sikku kolam*: one continuous line looping around the dots without ever ending. It can stand for the unbroken bond of marriage; this symbolism is an inference [M], so use the idea poetically rather than state it as fact.
  - *Padi kolam*: stepped geometric borders, traditional for auspicious days.
- Kolam is meant to be temporary and is walked over, so it is fine to use it on floors, thresholds, as a section divider or in a "drawn on" stroke animation.
- **Do not** put **உ** or deity names inside a kolam on a floor.
- Colour powder kolam (*rangoli*-style) is [M]. White rice-flour kolam is [H].

| Marigold (*samandhi*) | Used in Tamil Nadu, but it **reads North Indian when it dominates**. Keep it to an accent. | [D] |

## 7. Music and ceremonies

**Music: Mangala Vadhyam** [H]
- The *nadaswaram* is a long double-reed wind instrument with a flared bell. It is played in pairs alongside the *thavil*, a barrel drum played with a stick and thimbled fingers. A *sruti petti* (drone box) and cymbals may accompany them.
- **Getti melam** is the fast crescendo played at the exact moment the thaali is tied, so that no inauspicious sound is heard. It is a strong peak for the invitation's motion design.

**Event sequence.** Use this order. Rites marked "Brahmin" should be optional.

1. **Nichayathartham** (நிச்சயதார்த்தம்): engagement and exchange of *thamboolam*. Often held weeks earlier. [H]
2. **Pandhakaal Muhurtham** (பந்தக்கால் முகூர்த்தம்): erecting the first pandal post. [H]
3. **Sumangali Prarthanai**: prayer to married women ancestors. [H, Brahmin]
4. **Janavasam / Mappillai Azhaippu** (மாப்பிள்ளை அழைப்பு): the groom's welcome procession on the eve of the wedding. [H]
5. **Reception** (வரவேற்பு): **in modern Tamil Nadu it is usually held the evening before the muhurtham**, not after. [M]
6. **Vratham** and **Kasi Yathrai** (the groom's mock pilgrimage to Kashi, from which the bride's father turns him back). [H, Brahmin]
7. **Maalai Maatral** (exchange of garlands) and **Oonjal** (the swing). [H, Oonjal is mainly Brahmin]
8. **Kanyadhanam** (the bride is given away) → **Muhurtham / Mangalya Dharanam** (the thaali is tied, *getti melam* plays). [H]
9. **Saptapadi** (seven steps), **Ammi Midhithal** (stepping on the grinding stone, toe rings put on), **Arundhathi Paarthal** (showing the couple the star Arundhati). [H]
10. **Nalangu** (நலங்கு): playful games between the couple. [H]

Default set for the template: **Nichayathartham → Mappillai Azhaippu & Reception → Muhurtham → Nalangu**.

**Honourifics and naming**
- **Generic:** Thiru. (திரு.) / Thirumathi. (திருமதி.) for parents. Selvan (செல்வன்) for the groom and Selvi (செல்வி) for the bride. [H]
- **Brahmin style:** Chi. (சிரஞ்சீவி, *Chiranjeevi*) for the groom; Chi. La. Sow. (*Chiranjeevi Lakshmi Sowbhagyavathi*) for the bride; Sri. / Smt. for parents. [H]
- Name the family as "S/o" or "D/o" (son or daughter of), or as "grandson/granddaughter of" (*paeran/paethi*). Include the native place (*oor*), which matters in Tamil invitations. [H]
- **Sample names for placeholders:** Karthik and Meenakshi; Arvind and Janani; parents Thiru. Sundaram & Thirumathi. Valli.

**Blessing copy.** Keep the Tamil exact and give the English as a translation alongside it.
- "இறையருளாலும் பெரியோர்களின் ஆசியுடனும்…" — *With the grace of God and the blessings of our elders…* [H, standard opening]
- "தங்கள் வருகையை அன்புடன் எதிர்பார்க்கும்…" — *Lovingly awaiting your presence…* [H]
- "தாங்கள் தங்கள் சுற்றம் சூழ வந்திருந்து மணமக்களை வாழ்த்தியருள வேண்டுகிறோம்" — *We request you to come with your family and bless the couple.* [H]
- A Thirukkural couplet is acceptable as an epigraph. Quote it exactly and credit it with its number. [H]
- ⚠ Have a native Tamil speaker review all Tamil copy before release.

## 10. Elements to avoid

- **North Indian tropes:**
  - a baraat on horseback with dhol
  - *kalire*, *chooda*, *phulkari*
  - a sindoor-daan close-up
  - a "Haldi ceremony" with brass haldi bowls
  - sehra, mehndi as a headline event, lehenga as bridal wear
  - Punjabi or Rajasthani jharokhas
  - marigold-curtain overload
  - Mehndi and Sangeet now appear at many urban Tamil weddings [M], but they must not be presented as defaults.
- ⚠ **"Manjal Neerattu Vizha" is a coming-of-age ceremony for girls, not a wedding event.** Never use it as a "haldi" equivalent.
- **Kerala tropes:** kasavu cream-and-gold as the bridal look, Kathakali, snake boats, nilavilakku styling presented as Tamil. Kerala is a different tradition.
- **Mixing settings:** do not combine an agraharam and a Chettinad mansion in one scene, or a gopuram with a vimanam.
- **Misusing sacred symbols:**
  - **உ**, Om, a swastika or deity images as particles or tiled patterns
  - placing them on the floor
  - cropping them
  - deity images behind body text
- **Funerary palette:** all-white or white-and-black schemes with no turmeric or kumkum warmth read as mourning or widowhood. Black should only ever appear as a deep teak brown. A cream veshti with a gold border is fine.
- **Inauspicious or wrong imagery:** wilted flowers, extinguished or smoking lamps, broken coconuts shown carelessly, odd-looking or cracked vessels, owls.
- One generic thaali design presented as "the" Tamil thaali.
- Sanskritised North Indian wording ("Shubh Vivah", "Lagna Patrika") in place of Tamil terms.
- Misspelled Tamil, or Tamil text rendered as an image without review.

## 11. Recommended visual hierarchy

1. **The invocation crest:** **உ** plus the deity line, top-centre and alone. This sets the sacred register.
2. **The couple's names** inside a gopuram-tier or thiruvachi arch frame, in zari gold on arakku or ivory.
3. **The threshold scene:** Chettinad *thinnai* and *valavu* with Athangudi floor, a pair of lit *kuthuvilakku*, a *purna kumbham*, and plantain trees at the pillars. This scene is the world of the invitation.
4. **The flower layer:** malli and kanakambaram strands with a *mavilai thoranam* across the top edge.
5. **Textile borders:** Kanchi korvai bands with temple border, rudraksham and annam motifs as section dividers.
6. **Kolam:** threshold and section-break geometry, drawn on with a stroke animation.
7. **The muhurtham moment:** in the motion design, *getti melam* crescendos as the thaali or knot reveal happens.

## 12. Reference sources

- **Common knowledge (high confidence):** Tamil invitation conventions (the **உ** heading, family-deity line, *thiruvalar/selvan* naming, native place) as seen in printed Tamil kalyana pathirikai.
- **Geographical Indication registrations (high confidence):**
  - Kanchipuram silk (2005–06)
  - Madurai Malli (2013)
  - Athangudi tiles: GI status ⚠ not verified, so do not claim it.
- Chettinad heritage-mansion documentation (Karaikudi and Kanadukathan region); this region is also on UNESCO's tentative list as "Chettinad, Village Clusters of the Tamil Merchants".
- Standard ethnographic accounts of Iyer and Iyengar wedding rites (Vratham, Kasi Yathrai, Oonjal, Saptapadi, Ammi Midhithal, Arundhathi) and of the Nadaswaram and *getti melam* tradition.
- Hindu Marriage (Tamil Nadu Amendment) Act, 1967, which recognises Suyamariyathai marriages.
- **Inferences, not documented facts:** the symbolism of sikku kolam as an unbroken bond, and the reception-before-muhurtham norm (widely observed, but regional).
- **Not verified with a live web search in this pass.** Before release, verify the exact availability of the Tamil fonts and have a native speaker review the Tamil copy.
