# Cultural Art Direction Skill

Methodology for researching and documenting authentic cultural wedding traditions.

## Research Framework

### Primary Research Areas

1. **Architecture & Ceremonial Space**
   - Traditional wedding venues
   - Architectural motifs (arches, columns, domes, lattice)
   - Spatial arrangement of ceremonies

2. **Textiles & Fashion**
   - Bridal attire (regional variations matter)
   - Textile patterns (block print, embroidery, weaving, brocade)
   - Draping styles

3. **Jewelry & Metalwork**
   - Traditional wedding sets
   - Metalwork styles (Kundan, Meenakari, Temple, Thewa)
   - Ornamental chains, pendants, headpieces

4. **Botanicals**
   - Culturally significant flowers (jasmine, marigold, lotus, rose)
   - Garland styles (varmala, jaimala)
   - Foliage and arrangement traditions

5. **Traditional Art**
   - Regional art forms (Madhubani, Warli, Pichwai, Tanjore, Pattachitra, Kalamkari)
   - Pattern traditions (Kolam, Rangoli, Alpona, Mandala)
   - Calligraphy traditions (Devanagari, Arabic, Tamil, Gurmukhi)

6. **Ritual Objects & Symbols**
   - Ceremony-specific items
   - Auspicious symbols (Kalash, Ganesh, Om, crescent, etc.)
   - Fire, water, earth elements

7. **Color Significance**
   - Auspicious colors for this specific tradition
   - Colors to avoid and why
   - Regional color preferences

### Classification Requirements

Every element must be tagged:
- `[HISTORIC]` — documented traditional element
- `[MODERN]` — contemporary interpretation of tradition
- `[DECORATIVE]` — aesthetically fitting, not culturally specific

### Anti-patterns

- NEVER mix incompatible regional traditions (e.g., South Indian kolam with North Indian mehendi)
- NEVER use a religious symbol purely as decoration without understanding its significance
- NEVER assume "Indian wedding = red and gold" — traditions vary enormously
- NEVER use generic "ethnic" patterns

## Output Format

`planning/cultural-direction.md` with categorized elements, color rationale, font recommendations, and avoidance list.
