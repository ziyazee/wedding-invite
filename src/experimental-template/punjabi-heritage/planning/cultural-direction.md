## 1. Cultural overview

- **One region, shared folk layer.** A Punjabi wedding (*viah*, ਵਿਆਹ) has a core rite that differs by faith: Sikh families hold the *Anand Karaj*, and Hindu Punjabi families hold the *phere*. Almost everything around that rite belongs to **common Punjabi folk custom**. That includes *roka/shagun*, *maiyan* and *vatna*, *mehndi* with *giddha*, *jaago*, *chooda* and *kalire*, *ghodi* and *baraat*, *milni*, *doli*, and *pani vaarna*. Both communities practise these with the same songs, objects and jokes. [H]
- **This template builds only on that shared layer.** The core rite becomes a neutral, editable slot ("The Wedding" / ਵਿਆਹ). It shows no imagery of the gurdwara, the scripture, the sacred fire or the mandap. This is an **inclusive, blended** register, not a secular *legal* ceremony. The couple's actual rite still happens. The invitation simply doesn't picture it. [M]
- **Who the wedding belongs to.** In Punjabi custom, a wedding is a household and village event. The *naanke* (maternal family) arrive as a contingent called the *naanka mel* (ਨਾਨਕਾ ਮੇਲ). Neighbours lend *manje* (charpais) and bedding. Women's song traditions lead most of the pre-wedding days, and elders' blessings (*aseesan*) frame the whole occasion. [H]
- **Season: November to February.** Punjabi weddings cluster in winter, after the *kharif* rice harvest and the *rabi* sowing. [H] The look of that season:
  - **Light:** a low sun and short days. Sunset falls around 5:30 pm in December. Haze and dense ground fog (*dhund*, ਧੁੰਦ) soften the light, so dusk turns a diffused amber-rose, not the hard orange of a desert sunset. Long shadows fall across brick and lime plaster. [H]
  - **Fields:** *sarhon* (mustard) is in yellow bloom, mainly mid-December to February. **Wheat (*kanak*) is young and green in winter.** It only turns gold for the April harvest (Vaisakhi season). [H] See the season note in §6.
  - **Life:** shawls (*loi*, *khes*, phulkari shawls), braziers (*angithi*), *sarson da saag* with *makki di roti*, jaggery (*gurh*) and sugarcane season. [H]
- **The emotional arc runs from noise to tenderness to farewell to welcome.** It rises through teasing songs, the *dhol* and the night *jaago*, quiets at the dawn *chooda*, breaks at the *doli*, and resolves in laughter at *pani vaarna*. This template should hold the **warm, ancestral, golden-dusk** register throughout. It should feel like an heirloom, not a party flyer.

**Strict rule:** use only folk Punjabi and elders' blessing language. **Never** use scripture (Gurbani, Sanskrit mantras, Quranic text), invocations or faith-specific greetings.

**Poetic register**
- ***Suhaag*** (ਸੁਹਾਗ) are songs sung by the bride's women. They are tender, nostalgic and often addressed to the father (*babul*). [H]
- ***Ghodiyan*** (ਘੋੜੀਆਂ) are songs sung by the groom's sisters. They are proud and celebratory, about the mare and the groom's finery. [H]
- ***Sithniyan*** (ਸਿੱਠਣੀਆਂ) are teasing songs that the bride's women aim at the baraat. ***Boliyan*** (ਬੋਲੀਆਂ) are rhyming couplets sung in *giddha*. [H]
- Use these as **tonal guides**, for example "In the courtyard the suhaag has begun…". **Do not invent lyrics and present them as traditional.** If you quote, use only well-known public-domain folk lines, such as the *vidaai* song *"Saada chiṛiyan da chamba ve, babul asaan uḍ jaana"* ("We are a flock of sparrows, father; we will fly away") [H]. Keep that line for the closing only, because it is bittersweet.

**Must never appear:** ੴ (Ik Onkar), the Khanda, ਵਾਹਿਗੁਰੂ, ਸਤਿ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ (*Sat Sri Akal* is a faith greeting), *Ardaas*, *Anand Karaj* or *Laavan* as headings, any Gurbani line, ॐ, ਸ੍ਰੀ ਗਣੇਸ਼ਾਯ ਨਮਃ / श्री गणेशाय नमः, *Shubh Vivah* / *Lagna Patrika* (Hindi-Sanskrit register), swastika, *Ram Ram*, and "*Rab rakha*" ⚠ (folk, but it invokes God, so leave it out by default).

## 3. Vernacular Punjabi architecture & household heritage

**The setting is a modest-to-prosperous ancestral village haveli in Punjab plains brick vernacular.** It is warm, lived-in and dusty-gold. It is **not** a palace.

| Element | Punjabi | Visual notes | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gateway** | ਡਿਓੜੀ *deorhi* | A deep, covered entrance passage with a vaulted brick bay, sometimes with built-in brick benches (*thalle*) on both sides where elders sit. It is the hero frame of the opening, and the place for *milni*, *doli* and *pani vaarna*. | [H] |
| **Thin brick** | *nanakshahi itt* (ਨਾਨਕਸ਼ਾਹੀ ਇੱਟ) | Thin, flat, fired-clay bricks from late-18th/19th-century Punjab, close kin of the Mughal-era *lakhauri* brick. Show them as fine horizontal coursing in terracotta-rose, with lime mortar joints and worn edges. The name is an era term, not a religious symbol, so it is safe. | [H] |
| **Cusped arch** | *mehraab* | Multifoil (cusped) or pointed arches built **in brick**, sometimes lime-plastered and lightly painted. ⚠ Cusped arches are shared with Mughal architecture. Keep them in brick and lime, **never white marble**. | [H] |
| **Carved door** | ਟਾਹਲੀ *tahli* (sheesham) | A heavy double-leaf door of dark sheesham wood. It has carved panels of geometric rosettes, vines and lotus-less florals, iron or brass bosses (*kokke*, ਕੋਕੇ), a chain and hasp (*kundi / sangal*, ਕੁੰਡੀ, ਸੰਗਲ), and often a small wicket door. | [H] |
| **Courtyard** | ਵਿਹੜਾ *vehra* | An open court with lime-washed or mud-plastered (*lipai*, ਲਿਪਾਈ) walls and a clay-swept floor. It holds the *peerhi*, charpais and a hand pump (*nalka*). A **tahli, neem or *dhrek* tree** gives shade. | [H] |
| **Wall niche** | ਆਲਾ *aala* | A small arched wall niche for a lamp or a brass pot. It makes an ideal frame for names or event cards. | [H] |
| **Rooftop** | ਕੋਠਾ *kotha* | A flat roof with a low parapet (*banera*, ਬਨੇਰਾ). Women watch the lane from here. String lights (*lareeyan*), a few clay diyas and the fog over neighbouring roofs. | [H] / [M] for string lights |
| **Lane** | ਗਲੀ *gali* | A narrow brick-paved lane with open doorways and dung-cake walls. Keep it in the background only. | [H] |
| **Fields beyond** | ਸਰ੍ਹੋਂ ਦੇ ਖੇਤ *sarhon de khet* | Flat mustard fields to the horizon with a lone **kikar** (ਕਿੱਕਰ, *Vachellia nilotica*) or *tahli*, a tube-well hut (*koṭhi*), fog banks and a **brick-kiln chimney** silhouette far off. | [H] |

**Rules**
- **Never Rajasthani:** no pink or yellow sandstone, no stacked jharokha façades, no chhatris, no desert dunes, no camels.
- **Never Mughal:** no white marble, no onion domes, no *pietra dura*, no Taj silhouettes, no chandeliers.
- **Never the gurdwara:** no white domes with *kalash* finials, no Nishan Sahib, no sarovar.
- **Carving must contain no deities, no Ganesha on the lintel, no Om, no Khanda and no *kalash* finial.** Use only florals, vines, rosettes and geometric lattice.
- Late Sikh-period havelis often carry religious wall murals. **Do not reproduce them.** Plain lime wash with a painted border (*vel-boota*) is safe [H].
- ⚠ Old havelis sometimes have *thikri* (mirror-inlay) work. It reads Rajasthani and Mughal, so avoid it.

## 4. Folk ritual objects, vessels & heirlooms

| Object | Punjabi | Visual notes & use | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jaago pot** | ਜਾਗੋ ਦੀ ਗਾਗਰ / ਗੜਵੀ *gaagar / gadvi* | A brass or copper pitcher, decorated and topped with lit lamps made of kneaded dough or clay (*aate de deeve*). A woman of the *naanke* carries it on her head through the lanes on the night before the wedding. It becomes the **countdown**: its warm flames grow as the day nears. | [H] |
| **Belled stick** | ⚠ name varies (e.g. *khoonda* with *ghungroo*) | A stick hung with bells, shaken during the *jaago*. | [H] ⚠ name |
| **Low stool** | ਪੀੜ੍ਹੀ *peerhi* | A low wooden frame woven with cotton tape (*nivaar*) or *munj* cord, often painted. The bride or groom sits on it for *vatna*. | [H] |
| **Water pot** | ਘੜੋਲੀ *gharoli* | A small decorated pot, earthen or brass, tied with *mauli* (red-yellow thread) and wrapped in a red cloth. The groom's *bhabhi* fetches water in it for his bath. ⚠ Some families fetch from a gurdwara: never depict or name the source. | [H] |
| **Shagun tray** | ਸ਼ਗਨ ਦਾ ਥਾਲ | A brass tray of ***chhuare*** (ਛੁਹਾਰੇ, dried dates), *mishri*, dry fruit, a red envelope and a coil of ***mauli***. Used at roka and shagun. ⚠ Leave out the *tilak* and the coconut, since they are Hindu-ritual signals. | [H] |
| **Vatna bowl** | ਵਟਣਾ | A brass bowl of turmeric, besan, mustard oil and rosewater paste. A phulkari canopy is held over the bride at four corners. | [H] |
| **Wrist thread** | ਗਾਨਾ *gana* | Red thread with cowries, tied at *vatna* and untied later as a game. | [H] |
| **Bridal bangles** | ਚੂੜਾ *chooda* | Stacked **red and ivory** bangles (historically ivory, now acrylic), brought by the *mama*. The single strongest Punjabi bridal signifier. ⚠ Not Bengali *shakha-pola*. | [H] |
| **Dome ornaments** | ਕਲੀਰੇ *kalire* | Dangling umbrella or dome ornaments tied to the chooda. Older versions were made from dried coconut, *makhane* and cowries, and modern ones are gold-tone metal. The bride shakes them over unmarried girls: whoever a piece falls on "marries next". Natural fit for **RSVP**. | [H] (old) / [M] (metal) |
| **Drums** | ਢੋਲ · ਢੋਲਕੀ *dhol · dholki* | The ***dhol*** is a large double-headed barrel drum struck with the curved ***dagga*** (bass side) and the thin ***tilli*** (treble side). The ***dholki*** is smaller, hand-played and used at women's gatherings. | [H] |
| **Tongs** | ਚਿਮਟਾ *chimta* | Long iron tongs with brass jingle discs, a folk idiophone. | [H] |
| **Spinning wheel** | ਚਰਖਾ *charkha* | A painted wooden spinning wheel, sometimes inlaid with brass or bone. It was part of a bride's trousseau, and it is central to the ***trinjan*** (ਤ੍ਰਿੰਝਣ), the gathering of girls who spin together, a deep motif in Punjabi folk song. | [H] |
| **Hair tassel** | ਪਰਾਂਦਾ / ਪਰਾਂਦੀ *paranda* | A black silk braid extension ending in jewel-coloured tassels and pompoms (*phumman*), with *gota* work. Hang it as a vertical ornament beside names. | [H] |
| **Embroidered shoes** | ਤਿੱਲੇ ਵਾਲੀ ਜੁੱਤੀ *tilla jutti* | Leather slippers with gold-thread (*tilla*) embroidery and an upturned toe, associated with Patiala and Amritsar. | [H] |
| **Water vessel** | ਲੋਟਾ *lota* | A brass vessel used in ***pani vaarna***, where the groom's mother circles water over the couple at the threshold. Mustard oil is poured on the door-posts. | [H] |
| **Heirloom trunk** | ਸੰਦੂਕ / ਪੇਟੀ *sandook / peti* | A wooden trunk bound in brass, with painted panels. ⚠ **Do not label it *daaj* (dowry) in guest-facing copy.** Dowry is illegal and socially sensitive. Call it an "heirloom trunk". | [H] object, ⚠ wording |
| **Charpai** | ਮੰਜਾ *manja* | A woven cot. Neighbours traditionally lend *manje* for wedding guests. "A manja is kept for you" works as a warm RSVP idea. | [H] |
| **Puffed rice** | ਫੁੱਲੀਆਂ *phulian* | The bride throws it back over her head at the *doli*, a blessing so the house stays full. | [H] |

**Avoid as objects:** a havan kund or any sacred fire, the *kalash* with coconut, a tilak *thali* as a hero image, the *mangalsutra*, *sindoor*, the palla shown in a ceremonial context, the **kirpan** or **kara** shown in close-up (both are Sikh articles of faith), karah prashad, and a *kalgi* on the turban as a focal element ⚠ (it is associated with Guru Gobind Singh).

## 5. Textiles and craft

**Phulkari and Bagh** (ਫੁਲਕਾਰੀ · ਬਾਗ਼) are the signature textile. [H]
- **GI status:** Phulkari is GI-registered for Punjab, Haryana and parts of Rajasthan. ⚠ The registration year (believed to be about 2011) has not been verified, so do not print it.
- **Materials:** untwisted, floss-like ***pat*** silk on hand-spun, hand-woven cotton ***khaddar***. The khaddar is traditionally dyed madder red, rust, maroon or brown, and sometimes indigo. **White khaddar (*thirma*)** is associated with older women and widows, so do not use white as the ground. [H]
- **Stitch:** a long-and-short **darning stitch worked from the reverse**, counted thread by thread. Because it follows the weave, every motif is **geometric**: diamonds, lozenges, chevrons, stepped triangles, hexagons and eight-point stars. The floss catches light directionally, so one diamond looks two-toned as the angle changes. That makes a good motion cue (rotate the sheen, not the shape). [H]
- **Pat colours:** **golden-yellow and gold-orange** (dominant), crimson, rani pink, parrot green, white, and some deep blue. [H]

| Form | What it is | Use in the template | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phulkari** | Scattered motifs with the khaddar ground visible | Section backgrounds, light borders | [H] |
| **Bagh** ("garden") | Embroidery covers the whole ground | Hero panels, the couple frame | [H] |
| **Vari da bagh** | A wedding bagh given to the bride by the groom's family: golden-orange pat on red khaddar, dense diamond lattice | **The primary motif source.** Couple frame and celebration cards. | [H] |
| **Chope** | Given by the maternal grandmother around the bride's ritual bath. Red khaddar with borders worked in **double-running (Holbein) stitch**, which looks the same on both sides, so it is *not* darned. | Edge borders, the *vatna* event card | [H] |
| **Bawan bagh** | "Fifty-two" patterns: a sampler of 52 different blocks | The **celebrations grid**, one block per event | [H] |
| **Sainchi** | Figurative narrative phulkari from the Malwa region (Bathinda, Faridkot): people, animals, village scenes | Optional small vignettes. ⚠ Keep the figures folk and secular. | [H] |
| **Darshan dwar** | A "gateway" bagh made as a temple or gurdwara offering | **Avoid.** It is devotional by purpose. | [H] ⚠ |

**Other textiles and finishes**
- ***Gota-kinari*:** gold and silver ribbon borders on chunnis and suits. The craft is Rajasthani in origin but standard on Punjabi bridal wear. Use it as thin edge trims only, never as a Rajasthani leheriya or bandhani field. [M]
- ***Khes* and *durrie*:** hand-woven cotton bedcovers and floor rugs in checks and stripes, used on charpais in the vehra. [H]
- ***Tilla* and *zari* embroidery:** on juttis and the groom's achkan. [H]
- **Bridal wear:** a ***suha*** (red) or maroon salwar-kameez or lehenga with a phulkari or gota chunni over the head. The groom wears an achkan or sherwani with a *pagg* (turban). Depict the turban with respect, and never as caricature. [H]/[M]
- **The fake clip-art test:** if a "phulkari" contains curvy paisleys, freeform flowers, peacocks or satin-stitch shading, **it is not phulkari**. Rebuild it on a counted-thread grid so every edge snaps to 0°, 45° or 90°.

## 7. Music, folk dances & the event sequence

**Instruments** [H]
- ***Dhol*:** the baraat and bhangra drive, played in the bhangra *chaal* rhythm.
- ***Dholki*:** the heartbeat of women's gatherings.
- ***Chimta*:** iron tongs with jingles.
- ***Algoze*:** paired twin flutes.
- ***Tumbi*:** a one-string plucked lute, the sound of folk singers.
- Also the ***sapp*** (a wooden lazy-tongs clapper) and the ***kato*** (a squirrel-topped clapper stick).
- **Motion cue:** a single dhol *dagga* strike triggers the gateway opening. The tumbi carries ambient phrases.

**Dances** [H]
- ***Giddha*:** women's dance. Rhythmic clapping, a circle, and a soloist singing a *boli*.
- ***Bhangra*:** traditionally men's harvest dance, now mixed.
- ***Jhummar*:** a slow, graceful men's dance.
- ***Sammi*:** a women's dance from the Sandal Bar region of West Punjab.
- ***Luddi*:** a victory and celebration dance.
- ***Malwai giddha*:** a men's version, with chimta, sapp and kato.
- ⚠ Do not use Gujarati *garba* or *dandiya*, or Rajasthani *ghoomar*.

| 4 | **Jaago & Sangeet** | ਜਾਗੋ · ਸੰਗੀਤ | The *naanka mel* arrives, and the lamp-lit *gaagar* is paraded through the lanes. *Sangeet* (a staged music night) is the [M] urban form. | Jaago gaagar, chimta, belled stick | [H] / [M] |
| 5 | **Milni & The Wedding** | ਮਿਲਣੀ · ਵਿਆਹ | The two families meet in pairs at the *deorhi* and exchange shawls and garlands. The ceremony itself is **an editable slot with secular wording**: "The Wedding Ceremony", with a time and venue field. | Folded shawls, marigold haar; **chooda and kalire** as the bridal icon | [H] |
| 6 | **Reception / Doli** | ਡੋਲੀ | The *doli* farewell (phulian thrown back, *"Saada chiṛiyan da chamba"*), then the warm welcome at the groom's door with *pani vaarna*. A modern *reception* may be added [M]. | Brass lota, phulian thaal, the deorhi again | [H] / [M] |

- **Wording rule for event 5:** use "The Wedding Ceremony" / ਵਿਆਹ ਦੀ ਰਸਮ. Do not use "Anand Karaj", "Laavan", "Phere", "Pheras" or "Saat Phere" unless the couple types them in themselves. Leave the default time empty, because Sikh ceremonies are in the morning and Hindu ones usually in the evening or at night. [M]
- Optional extras the couple can switch on: *Chooda* ceremony, *Sehrabandi & Ghodi*, *Baraat*, *Pani Vaarna*. [H]

**Naming conventions**
- Invitations come from the **parents, often with the grandparents named first**, in the form "son/daughter of … grandson/granddaughter of …" (ਪੋਤਰਾ/ਪੋਤਰੀ, ਦੋਹਤਾ/ਦੋਹਤੀ). [H]
- **Native place matters:** "Pind [village], Tehsil/District [X], Punjab", or the city, sometimes alongside the current diaspora city (for example "Pind Dhudike, Moga · now Brampton"). [H] Give this its own field.
- **Honorifics:** for an inclusive default, use English *Mr. & Mrs.*, or Punjabi ਸ੍ਰੀ / ਸ੍ਰੀਮਤੀ ⚠. *Sardar/Sardarni* (ਸ./ਸ੍ਰੀਮਤੀ) is Sikh-specific, so offer it as an opt-in. [H]
- Clan surnames such as Sandhu, Gill, Grewal, Sidhu, Bajwa, Dhillon, Ahluwalia, Khanna, Malhotra, Kapoor and Sethi are used across communities. ⚠ **Never add caste or *got* labels** (for example "Jatt") to copy.
- **Placeholder names (faith-neutral):**
  - Couples: **Arjun & Simran**, **Kabir & Mehar**
  - Parents: **Mr. Rajinder & Mrs. Harjeet Sandhu**, **Mr. Ashok & Mrs. Neelam Khanna**
  - Native places: **Pind Ghanaur, Patiala** or **Jalandhar**

**Welcome copy (English + optional Gurmukhi).** ⚠ Every Gurmukhi string needs a native reader's review before release.
- ਜੀ ਆਇਆਂ ਨੂੰ — *"Jee Aayan Nu. The doors of our home are open, and the courtyard awaits you."* [H heading, M sentence]
- ਵੱਡਿਆਂ ਦੀਆਂ ਅਸੀਸਾਂ ਨਾਲ… — *"With the blessings of our elders, we invite you to the wedding of our beloved…"* [H]
- ਆਪ ਜੀ ਨੂੰ ਪਰਿਵਾਰ ਸਮੇਤ ਸੱਦਾ ਦਿੱਤਾ ਜਾਂਦਾ ਹੈ — *"You and your family are warmly invited."* [H, a standard card line]
- ਆਪ ਜੀ ਦੀ ਹਾਜ਼ਰੀ ਸਾਡੇ ਲਈ ਮਾਣ ਵਾਲੀ ਗੱਲ ਹੋਵੇਗੀ — *"Your presence will be our honour."* [M]
- ਤੁਹਾਡੀ ਉਡੀਕ ਰਹੇਗੀ — *"We will be waiting for you."* (RSVP / closing) [M]
- **Countdown line:** *"The gaagar is being lit — the jaago is coming."* (English; *"Jaago aayi aa"* is the traditional call) [H call, M line]
- **RSVP line:** *"A manja is kept for you in the vehra. Tell us you'll come."* [M, rooted in the practice of borrowing manje]

## 8. Colour palette

**Anchor: a warm golden-amber dusk on brick, phulkari and brass.** There is no pale pink, no navy night ground and no pure black or white.

| Name | Hex | Cultural reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Khaddar parchment** | `#F1E2C3` | Unbleached hand-spun cotton and lime plaster in dusk light. **Primary ground.** |
| **Chooda ivory** | `#F7EEDC` | The ivory half of the chooda. Highlights and type on dark grounds. Never `#FFF`. |
| **Aathan glow** (ਆਥਣ, evening) | `#E8914B` | A winter-dusk sky diffused through *dhund*. Sky gradients and rim light. |
| **Pat-silk gold** | `#E2A12F` | The dominant golden-yellow floss of the *bagh*. Motifs, rules, display accents. |
| **Sarhon mustard** | `#D8B21E` | Mustard bloom. Field bands and small floral accents. Use it sparingly next to pat gold so the two stay distinct. |
| **Brass gaagar** | `#AF8436` | The jaago pot, lota and door bosses. Metallic shading on gold. |
| **Suha crimson** | `#9C1D2B` | *Suha* is the auspicious red of folk song: the chooda red, bridal suit and chunni. **Primary accent.** |
| **Manjith madder** | `#6C1624` | Madder-dyed khaddar under the *vari da bagh*. Deep panels and the couple frame ground. |
| **Nanakshahi terracotta** | `#A5532F` | Fired thin brick. The *deorhi*, arches and architecture. |
| **Mehndi olive-bronze** | `#6E6A2F` | Wet henna, kikar leaves and green winter *kanak* in shadow. Secondary botanical tone. |
| **Kanak straw** | `#D6B97C` | Dried wheat sheaves and *munj* cord. A soft mid-tone for borders and dividers. |
| **Tahli walnut ink** | `#3B2519` | Sheesham doors, carved shadow and body text. **The darkest value. Replaces black.** |
| *(optional)* **Dhund haze** | `#BFAE9F` | Winter fog over fields. Atmospheric depth layers only. |

- **Pairing logic:** follow the *bagh*. Gold sits on madder or crimson, framed by terracotta brick and lit by amber sky. Green (olive and kanak) appears only as a quiet field counter-note.
- **Contrast:** use walnut ink on parchment for body text (high contrast). On madder panels, use ivory or pat gold. Avoid gold text on terracotta, because the contrast is too low.
- **Avoid:**
  - **Saffron or *kesri* as the brand colour**, because it carries Sikh religious weight. Allow it only as a tiny accent, if at all.
  - Blush pink `#fdf6f4` and rosewater pastels, which belong to `punjabi-wedding`.
  - Navy night `#1C2140`, which belongs to `punjabi-journey`.
  - All-white or white-and-black schemes, which read as mourning, or as *thirma* for widows.

## 9. Typography

| Role | Google Fonts | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Latin display** | **Fraunces** (soft, warm, optical-size serif; use the high-contrast "SOFT" axis at large sizes) or **Gloock** (stately, high-contrast) | Couple names and section titles. It should feel like a heritage letterpress, not a luxury fashion brand. |
| **Latin accent caps** | **Marcellus SC** | Small engraved-feeling labels: event names, dates, "JEE AAYAN NU". Track it wide. |
| **Latin body** | **Spectral** or **EB Garamond** | Crisp, readable serif for details. Use 16 px or larger on mobile. |
| **Gurmukhi display** | **Tiro Gurmukhi** | A traditional serif with good stroke contrast, for secular greetings and names. |
| **Gurmukhi text** | **Noto Serif Gurmukhi** or **Mukta Mahee** | Small Gurmukhi lines. |
| **Gurmukhi festive (sparingly)** | **Baloo Paaji 2** | Rounded and playful. Use only for a *balle balle* style accent, never for headings. |

- ⚠ Check rendering of the subjoined *haha* (੍ਹ in ਸਰ੍ਹੋਂ, ਪੀੜ੍ਹੀ), the *addak* (ੱ in ਸੱਦਾ, ਜੁੱਤੀ), the *tippi* and *bindi* (ਂ ੰ in ਨੂੰ), and the nukta letters (ਸ਼ ਜ਼ ਗ਼). Confirm current weights and availability for each font.
- Gurmukhi greetings are a **crest-level accent, set above or beside the English**, not the main reading text. Render them as live text, not images.
- Never fake Gurmukhi with a "Punjabi-looking" Latin font, and never use the Ik Onkar glyph as a decorative initial.

## 10. Hard rules — elements to avoid

**A. Zero religious symbols or scripture (non-negotiable)**
- **Sikh:** no ੴ Ik Onkar, Khanda, Nishan Sahib, Guru Granth Sahib, palki, chaur, rumala, gurdwara darbar or exterior domes, Gurus' portraits or silhouettes, Gurbani or Laavan text, *Waheguru*, *Sat Sri Akal* headings, karah prashad, or kirpan/kara close-ups.
- **Hindu:** no Ganesha, Om, swastika, deities, *kalash* with coconut, havan or sacred fire, mandap or *vedi*, sindoor-daan, mangalsutra, Sanskrit mantras, or *Shri Ganeshaya Namah*.
- **Muslim / other:** no Quranic text or crescent motifs.
- **Neutral ground:** diyas and lamplight are allowed **as folk light** (on the jaago gaagar, in wall niches, along the kotha), never as worship (*aarti* plates, lamps before an image).

**B. No regional mix-ups**
- **Rajasthan:** sandstone palaces, jharokha façades, chhatris, camels, dunes, puppets, *ghoomar*, bandhani or leheriya fields, peacocks everywhere, and *thikri* mirror work.
- **Mughal:** white marble, onion domes, Taj silhouettes, *pietra dura*, chandelier ballrooms.
- **South India:** banana-tree entrances, kolam, jasmine braids, nadaswaram, gopurams, Kanchipuram silk.
- **Bengal:** *shakha-pola* passed off as the chooda, *alpona*, *topor*.
- **Gujarat:** garba or dandiya at the sangeet.
- **Other:** Lohri bonfire *offerings* (Lohri is a January festival, not a wedding rite; a warm brazier for winter flavour is fine).

**C. No Bollywood caricature**
- A generic Bollywood sangeet stage, neon "Balle Balle" typography, cartoon turbaned uncles, beard or turban jokes, tractor-and-gun "jatt" machismo, alcohol bottles, hookahs or cigarettes, and "Mehndi Laga Ke Rakhna" pastiche.

**D. No fake phulkari**
- No freeform florals, paisleys, peacocks or satin shading called "phulkari". No phulkari on white grounds. No random tiling without a counted-grid logic.

**E. Other sensitivities**
- Do not call the trunk *daaj* (dowry), and never imply gifts or dowry are expected.
- No caste or *got* labels.
- "Sada suhagan raho" is opt-in only.
- No crying-bride melodrama as the hero image. Keep the *doli* tender and warm.
- **Seasonal accuracy:** no ripe golden wheat field beside blooming mustard, and no summer-harvest cues in a winter invitation.
- No inauspicious imagery: extinguished or smoking lamps, wilted garlands, cracked vessels, owls.

## 12. Reference sources & verification notes

**High-confidence common knowledge**
- Shared Punjabi folk wedding sequence: roka/shagun, maiyan/vatna, mehndi/giddha, jaago, chooda/kalire, sehra/ghodi, milni, doli, pani vaarna.
- The suhaag, ghodiyan, sithniyan and boliyan song genres.
- The November–February wedding season, the mustard bloom calendar and the April wheat harvest.

**Craft and textile history**
- Documented phulkari and bagh scholarship, including museum collections of Punjab phulkari (for example the Victoria and Albert Museum, the Philadelphia Museum of Art, and the Calico Museum, Ahmedabad).
- Types *vari da bagh*, *chope*, *bawan bagh*, *sainchi*, *thirma* and *darshan dwar*.
- Darning stitch from the reverse on khaddar with *pat* floss, and double-running stitch for *chope*.

**Vernacular architecture**
- Punjab haveli and *deorhi* typology, *nanakshahi* and *lakhauri* thin brick, and carved sheesham doors (Punjab and Chiniot woodwork traditions).
- Cross-checked against `src/experimental-template/punjabi-journey/planning/research.md`.

**Structure**
- Modelled on `src/experimental-template/tamil-wedding/planning/cultural-direction.md`.

**Inferences and choices, not documented facts**
- The *jaago gaagar* as a countdown, *kalire* as the RSVP, and *bawan bagh* as an event grid are creative mappings [M] derived from ritual logic.
- Treating dried golden wheat sheaves as a decorative harvest keepsake is a [D] choice made to reconcile the brief with the season.

**Verify before release (⚠)**
1. Every Gurmukhi string, by a native Punjabi reader.
2. The phulkari GI registration year and registered regions, before stating either.
3. The *siris*-leaf doorway custom and its regional spread.
4. The local name of the *jaago* belled stick.
5. The availability and weights of Tiro Gurmukhi, Noto Serif Gurmukhi, Mukta Mahee, Baloo Paaji 2, Fraunces, Gloock, Marcellus SC and Spectral.
6. Have one Sikh and one Hindu Punjabi family member each confirm that nothing in the final build reads as religious.
